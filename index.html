About Junhao Ye

Hi! I am Junhao Ye (叶俊豪), a Ph.D. student in the School of Integrated Circuits at Southeast University, advised by Prof. Zhe Jiang. I work at the intersection of large language models and electronic design automation, building practical methods that make RTL verification more automated, scalable, and dependable. My work has led to a first-author paper at ICCAD and a CCF-A paper at DAC, and I enjoy turning research prototypes into tools that can be used by hardware engineers.

My current research develops LLM-aided verification systems for UVM testbench generation, assertion generation, and RTL security analysis. Beyond individual papers, I care about the full path from design intent to executable verification, including feedback, debugging, and deployment at IP and subsystem level. Alongside research, I have received the National Scholarship, Excellent Star of SZU, and Jicui Graduate recognition, while continuing to grow through open-source work and public-facing projects.

Research 研究方向

Verification is the dominant front-end bottleneck: as designs become more integrated, the verification burden grows faster than design complexity. My work explores LLM-aided ways to make this process more scalable and trustworthy.

Verification: The Dominant Front-End Bottleneck

The bottleneck is not only writing RTL; it is building, debugging, and scaling the verification machinery around it.

LLM-AIDED DIRECTIONS

Three complementary directions target the most expensive parts of verification workflows.

01

UVM-TB Gen

Generate reusable UVM environments from RTL, specifications, and protocol knowledge, then refine them through simulation feedback.

↗ 02

Assert-Gen

Turn design intent and RTL context into executable SystemVerilog Assertions for formal checking and traceable verification.

↗ 03

Security

Discover RTL security vulnerabilities across specifications and module boundaries, then validate candidates with formal methods and simulation.

Experience 实习经历

EDARESEARCH INTERN

Research Intern · National Center for Technology Innovation in EDA

Worked on LLM-aided UVM testbench generation, decomposing IP-level verification into component-wise generation, iterative error checking, repair, and stimulus enhancement. Built and evaluated a workflow that connects RTL, specifications, protocol context, simulation feedback, and debugging into one usable verification process.

The resulting workflow was integrated as a verification tool in ChatDV (project note), enabling small IP-level UVM verification.
